Best CMS for small businesses in 2027: how to choose

No single CMS suits every small business, so this guide starts with the questions that decide the choice. If you want nothing to run yourself, a hosted builder fits. If you need a shop with payments, pick a platform made for selling. If you want your own site on your own server, with bookings, forms and a newsletter included, Talea is one option, and this page says where it falls short.

Last checked 31 October 2026

Five questions that decide the choice

  1. Who keeps it running? A hosted builder is run by its vendor. A self-hosted system is run by you or by whoever installed it, which means updates, backups and a server.
  2. Do you need to take payments? Shops and paid memberships need checkout. Check this first, because it rules some systems out at once.
  3. What must be included? Contact forms, bookings, a newsletter and members-only pages are either part of the product or something you add.
  4. Can you leave with your content? Look at what each product exports before you build a site in it.
  5. What does it cost per year? Add the subscription or the server, the domain, and the time somebody spends on upkeep.

These questions matter more than a list of features. A business where nobody has time for upkeep gets more from a hosted plan than from a server. A business with a freelancer or a technical friend on call has more options.

What each option does well

  • WordPress offers a very large choice of plugins and themes, runs on PHP hosting that meets its requirements, and has an installer. Minor core updates apply automatically by default.
  • Joomla lists multilingual sites and several levels of user permissions among its strengths.
  • Ghost makes memberships, subscriptions and newsletters the core of the product.
  • Grav and Kirby keep content in files, which takes the database off the list of things to run.
  • Squarespace and Wix leave nothing to run yourself, and support is included. Wix has bookings built in. Squarespace sells appointment booking as a separate product, Acuity Scheduling.

What running your own site costs

Software that is free to download still costs money and time. You pay for a server and a domain, somebody applies updates, and backups need a destination. A hosted plan folds these into one fee. This page gives no hosting prices, because they depend on the provider you choose.

Ghost's pricing page makes the same point about its own product: if you self-host, the upkeep is yours. The same holds for every system in the first table, Talea included.

Where Talea fits

Talea is a website builder for your own server. It comes with 14 finished looks, 40 ready-made sections and editing right on the page. Bookings, forms, a newsletter, members-only pages and versions of the site in 45 languages are part of it, so there are no plugins to choose.

An update is a new container image, and backups go to FTPS or S3 on a schedule. The whole site exports as an open archive. Websites for several locations can run from one installation. Assistants such as Claude can connect over MCP, and for each connection you decide whether the assistant may publish or only write drafts.

Talea fits a business whose site is set up once by a freelancer, an agency or a technical friend and then edited by the owner. The other guides go deeper: Talea as a WordPress alternative, as a Squarespace or Wix alternative, for several locations, and for bookings.

What Talea does not do

  • Talea has no checkout and no payments. You can show products with prices and variants and collect order enquiries in a basket.
  • Talea has no updater in the admin and does not run on plain shared hosting. It runs in Docker, and an update means pulling a new image.
  • Talea has no large plugin or theme ecosystem. You get 14 looks and 40 sections instead of thousands of templates.
  • Members sign in with an e-mailed link. Paid memberships are not included.
  • The admin comes in English, German and Czech.

Which one to choose, by situation

  • You want nothing to run and accept a monthly fee: a hosted builder such as Squarespace or Wix.
  • You need an online shop with payments: a platform made for selling. Compare checkout features before anything else.
  • You want a very large choice of plugins and themes, or your developer already works in it: WordPress.
  • Newsletters and paid memberships are the product: Ghost.
  • You are a developer who wants content in files: Grav or Kirby.
  • You want your own site on your own server, with bookings, forms and a newsletter included, handed to the owner after setup: Talea.

Questions people ask

What is the best CMS for a small business in 2027?+

It depends on who keeps the site running. A hosted builder suits a business with no one for upkeep, a shop platform suits a business that sells online, WordPress suits a business that wants a very large choice of plugins and themes, and Talea suits a business that wants its own server with bookings, forms and a newsletter included.

Which CMS can I host myself?+

WordPress, Joomla, Ghost, Grav, Kirby and Talea can all run on your own server. Their requirements differ: Grav needs no database, Ghost needs Node.js and MySQL, WordPress and Joomla need PHP 8.3 or newer, and Talea runs in Docker.

Do I have to update a self-hosted CMS myself?+

Yes, or someone you ask has to. WordPress applies minor core updates automatically by default, and plugins and themes update automatically only in special cases. A Talea update is a new container image that the person running the server pulls.

Can I leave Wix or Squarespace later?+

Squarespace exports an XML file in WordPress format that leaves out several page types, drafts and style settings. Wix states that a Wix site must be hosted and operated on its own servers. Talea's website import can read a public site by its address and rebuild the pages.

Is Talea free?+

Talea is free software under GPL-2.0-or-later. You pay for the server and the domain, and the upkeep is yours or that of the person you ask.

Can I sell online with Talea?+

You can show products with prices and variants and collect order enquiries in a basket. Checkout and payments are not built in.
